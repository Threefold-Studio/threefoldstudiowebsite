Threefold Studio

A one thumb school pickup puzzle.

Everybody gets home. Nobody goes in the water.

In development

It is home time at an island school, and the parents arrive by pontoon boat. Send each student down the dock to the right boat, in an order that works, without loading one side so heavily that the whole thing tips.

The idea

One tap at a time.

Tap the student at the front of a line and they walk down to whichever boat is at the dock. If it is not their family's boat, they sit on the bench and wait, and you tap them again when the right one pulls in. The bench only holds so many.

Siblings stand together and leave together, which is either a gift or a problem depending on how much room is left.

The part that makes it a puzzle

A pontoon boat can tip.

Students board the side of the boat facing their own yard, so you are not just choosing who goes next. You are choosing which side of the boat gets heavier.

Two more on one side and it starts to leak. You have three moves to even it out. Three more and it goes under.

It turns a queueing puzzle into a balancing one. The right student at the wrong moment sinks the boat, and the bench is where you park that problem until the other side catches up.

What turns up later

The days get harder.

  • ●Students in gray hoodies keep their color hidden until they reach the front of the line.
  • ●Bigger boats, eight seats instead of six, which is more room and more ways to get the weight wrong.
  • ●The Late Bell. Some days are timed, and everybody has to be off the island before it rings.
How much of it there is

Five hundred levels at launch.

Every level is a school day, and fifty days make a grade. You start in Preschool, move up through Kindergarten, and keep going one year at a time, so five hundred days carries a player from their very first morning to the end of 8th grade. It is a progress bar that already means something to anybody who has ever sat in a pickup line.

Still being built.
